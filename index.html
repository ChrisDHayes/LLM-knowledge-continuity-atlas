What survives when the model changes?

What is this about?

AI can help us reach a conclusion, but the conclusion alone doesn't tell the whole story. This experience explores what happens to the facts, reasons, objections and tests behind it when an organization changes AI models.

The big question: can a company keep what it learned—even when the AI helping it changes?

Why I built this

I created this Atlas to make a hidden problem easier to see: keeping an AI-generated answer is not the same as keeping the reasons for trusting it. The intention is to help people see what must be recorded, handed over, challenged and checked again when an AI system changes.

Questions this experience helps answer

What did we never write down? What is missing versus unknown? What survives a handover? Can we reconstruct why a decision was made? What if the saved assumption was wrong? What must the replacement AI be tested on, and who needs to authorise its use?

How to read this exhibit: The coloured flow is a teaching example. Only the research section displays published study results.

      Why this matters

      Capture, transfer, retrieval, interpretation, validity and authority are separate questions. A better handover cannot create absent records. A complete record can preserve an error. A new AI still needs its own task-specific checks and current permission to act.

      Evidence, assumptions and limits

      The service-team story, records, owners, recording states, handover outcomes and change events are fictional teaching examples. They are not measured organisational rates, causal estimates or maturity ratings. Equal ribbon widths do not represent reliability or probability. The chart stops at availability; it does not demonstrate retrieval, understanding, correct behaviour or permission. Published research and its caveats remain in the research section.
